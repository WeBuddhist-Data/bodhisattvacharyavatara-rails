# Day 12 — Story — Legkye's Story

**Core theme:** The power of selfless compassion beyond expectation

**Concept:** "The Bodhisattva's kindness is not a transaction but a natural flow"

## Version 1
### Opening (0-10 sec)

- Extreme close up of a hand holding a small coin, then letting it drop into a jar with a loud clink

**Voiceover**
We are taught to praise those who return a kindness. If someone helps you and you pay them back, that is honorable. But what if the kindness begins with you?

### Middle (10-50 sec)
**On screen**
- Soft, warm lighting on the creator. The creator uses hand gestures to tell the story of the young boy Legkye
- Shots of dry, barren wilderness transitioning into a flourishing garden

**Voiceover**
Consider the story of a young boy named Legkye. Fleeing danger with his parents, they had no food left. His mother was desperate, ready to sacrifice him to survive. But instead of fear, Legkye felt compassion. He offered his own flesh to sustain them, little by little. He did not ask for a reward or even a thank you. He simply acted. Even when tested by gods in the form of beggars and beasts, he held no regret. His heart was so pure that his body was restored instantly. This is not just a legend. It is a mirror for us. Most of us give to get. We give to be liked, to be seen, or to ensure someone owes us later. But the bodhisattva acts differently. They are not moved by what they can gain or by the social contract of debt. They are moved by a natural, deep love for all beings that just flows out, unasked and unstoppable. They look at the world and see no strangers, only beings in need of happiness.

### End (50-60 sec)
**On screen**
- Creator looking away from the camera, then back with a gentle, thoughtful expression

**Voiceover**
Next time you reach out to help, ask yourself. Am I waiting for something in return? Or am I ready to be as free as the one who gives without being asked?

**On screen**

- Slow panning shots of a serene statue of a peaceful figure

**Voiceover**
Imagine a seven year old boy named Legkye. In the middle of a brutal journey, his starving parents considered the unthinkable to survive. Legkye did not wait to be asked. He gave his own blood and flesh to keep them alive. He did it without expectation, without hope for glory, and without a single moment of regret. This is the definition of a bodhisattva. Many of us are stuck in a world of trade. You give to me, so I owe you. It is a small, limited way to live. But think about the scale of Legkye's act. He did not care about his own body. He did not care about the pain. He only cared that his parents might live. When we operate from that same source of natural compassion, our kindness stops being a transaction. It stops being a way to balance the books. Instead, it becomes a spontaneous gift that benefits everyone around us, no matter who they are or what they have done for us in the past.

You see a force of nature. He was not looking for a pat on the back. His compassion was not a reaction to someone else's kindness; it was the essence of who he was. When he offered his own life, he did it with total freedom. He taught us that the highest form of virtue is simply to give because you can, because someone needs you, and because the love in your heart is deeper than any desire for return.
