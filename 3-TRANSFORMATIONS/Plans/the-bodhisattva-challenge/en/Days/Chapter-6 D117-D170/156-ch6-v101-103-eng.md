## Today's Verse

> I desire to enter suffering,
> and like the Buddhas' blessings,
> they are the very gateway that I do not abandon.
> How can I be angry at them? ^6-101

> It is inappropriate to be angry at them,
> thinking, "This hinders my good deeds."
> If there is no austerity equal to patience,
> do I not rely on them? ^6-102

> If I, due to my own faults,
> do not practice patience towards them,
> then I merely hinder
> this cause of good deeds. ^6-103

## 1) Introduction to Today's Practice

Today's practice is based on the one hundred and first, one hundred and second, and one hundred and third verses of the sixth chapter. Today, is the person who stops you a gate gently closed, so that you do not go on the path of suffering?

## 2) Commentary Explanation

The commentaries on these verses give us the following example, which is easy to understand and encouraging. Those who are attached to gains and praise want to go on the path of suffering of the lower realms. Someone who cuts off our gains is like a gate or a door blessed by the Buddha. They do not let us go on that bad path. So it is not right to be angry at the one who cuts off our gains. There is no austerity and no good action greater than patience. So we must stay in the austerity of patience.

## 3) Today's Practice

**Actual Practice:** Today, when other people make me angry, I will meditate on patience and keep my mind peaceful.

**Explanation:** _(Patience Practice)_ Patience is the best austerity. The harm others do becomes a friend that helps us gather merit. This clears away anger, and we gain peace.
