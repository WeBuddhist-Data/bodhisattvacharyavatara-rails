## Today's Verse

> Even if a king is angry,
> does the harm of hell
> that one experiences by displeasing sentient beings
> cause that? ^6-131

> Even if a king is pleased,
> the buddhahood that one obtains
> by pleasing sentient beings
> is impossible to be granted. ^6-132

## 1) Introduction to Today's Practice

Today's practice is based on the one hundred and thirty-first and one hundred and thirty-second verses of the sixth chapter. Today, whom are you trying to make happy, and whom are you secretly discouraging?

## 2) Commentary Explanation

The commentaries on these verses give us the following point, which is easy to understand and encouraging. Even if a fierce king gets very angry, he cannot send us to hell. But if we make sentient beings unhappy, we have to experience the suffering of hell. Even if a king is very pleased, he can give only food and wealth, not the state of a buddha. If a bodhisattva pleases sentient beings, the bodhisattva will gain the happiness of the higher realms and of buddhahood.

## 3) Today's Practice

**Actual Practice:** Today, I will not hurt others or make them sad.

**Explanation:** _(Avoiding wrongdoing)_ If we make sentient beings unhappy, we will experience the harm of hell. If we meditate on loving-kindness for others, happiness comes to both ourselves and others. This is the root of merit.
