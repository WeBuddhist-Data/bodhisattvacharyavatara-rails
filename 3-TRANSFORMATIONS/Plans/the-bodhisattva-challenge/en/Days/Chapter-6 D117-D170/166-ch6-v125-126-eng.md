## Today's Verse

> To please the Buddhas,
> from now on, I will be a servant to the world.
> May the Protectors of the world be pleased,
> even if the multitude of beings step on my head or kill me, I will not resist. ^6-125

> There is no doubt that these beings,
> whose nature is compassion, are considered my self.
> These very beings, seen as my self—
> why should I not respectfully serve that Protector? ^6-126

## 1) Introduction to Today's Practice

Today's practice is based on the one hundred and twenty-fifth and one hundred and twenty-sixth verses of the sixth chapter. If you became a servant to every person you meet today, what would you do differently?

## 2) Commentary Explanation

The commentaries on these verses give us the following point, which is easy to understand and encouraging. To please the buddhas, from today on we must give up our anger and pride and serve all sentient beings like a servant. Even if many sentient beings hit us on the head with stones or kill us, we do not strike back. From the very beginning, the Buddha, who is compassion itself, has held those sentient beings to be one with himself. So we must respect sentient beings, who appear in the form of that protector.

## 3) Today's Practice

**Actual Practice:** Today, even if other people say bad words to me, I will help them in a peaceful way.

**Explanation:** _(Patience Practice)_ If we meditate on patience like this, the buddhas become pleased. It also calms our own hatred and suffering. Serving all beings like a servant is the best thing to do.
