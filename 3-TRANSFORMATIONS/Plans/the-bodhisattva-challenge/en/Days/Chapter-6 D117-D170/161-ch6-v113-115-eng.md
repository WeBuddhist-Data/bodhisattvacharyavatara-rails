## Today's Verse

> Sentient beings and the Buddhas
> are similar in accomplishing buddhahood.
> Why then should one show respect to the Buddhas
> but not to sentient beings? ^6-113

> The good qualities of intention are not inherent;
> they come from the result. Therefore, similarly,
> sentient beings also have good qualities.
> Therefore, they are equal. ^6-114

> Whatever offering is made to those with loving-kindness
> is the greatness of sentient beings.
> Whatever merit is generated by faith in the Buddhas
> is also the greatness of the Buddhas. ^6-115

## 1) Introduction to Today's Practice

Today's practice is based on the one hundred and thirteenth, one hundred and fourteenth, and one hundred and fifteenth verses of the sixth chapter. Even though bowing to a statue of the Buddha is easy for us, why is it hard to show respect to the people around us?

## 2) Commentary Explanation

The commentaries on these verses give us the following point, which is easy to understand and encouraging. The buddhas and sentient beings are not equal in their intentions and good qualities. But sentient beings have a good quality: they help us reach awakening. So in helping us reach awakening, the buddhas and sentient beings are the same. Just as we show respect to the buddhas, we must also show respect to sentient beings.

## 3) Today's Practice

**Actual Practice:** Today, I will respect everyone I meet as I would a buddha, and I will speak gently.

**Explanation:** _(Doing good)_ If we respect sentient beings, we achieve the qualities of a buddha. This calms our own mind, and great benefit comes. This is the path of seeing the buddhas and sentient beings as equal.
