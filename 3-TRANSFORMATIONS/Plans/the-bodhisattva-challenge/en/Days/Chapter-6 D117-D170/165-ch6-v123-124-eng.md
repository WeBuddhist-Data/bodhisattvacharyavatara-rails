## Today's Verse

> Just as a body ablaze with fire
> cannot be at ease with any desire,
> likewise, when sentient beings are harmed,
> the Great Compassionate Ones have no way to rejoice. ^6-123

> Therefore, whatever displeasure
> the Great Compassionate Ones feel because I have harmed beings,
> I confess that misdeed today.
> May the Buddhas forgive whatever displeasure there was. ^6-124

## 1) Introduction to Today's Practice

Today's practice is based on the one hundred and twenty-third and one hundred and twenty-fourth verses of the sixth chapter. Today, if we confess all the harm we have done to others, is there harm you did to another person recently that you have not yet put right?

## 2) Commentary Explanation

The commentaries on these verses give us the following example, which is easy to understand and encouraging. If a person's whole body is on fire, giving them everything they want cannot make them happy. In the same way, when harm reaches sentient beings, those who are full of compassion cannot be happy either. Because of our ignorance, we harmed sentient beings and made those who are full of compassion unhappy. So today we confess that mistake. We ask the buddhas to forgive everything that has displeased them.

## 3) Today's Practice

**Actual Practice:** Today, when I remember harm I did to others, I will confess it with regret and ask for forgiveness.

**Explanation:** _(Avoiding wrongdoing)_ If we confess our own mistakes, the mind becomes pure. If we give up harming others, the buddhas are pleased. This is the best way to grow love for beings.
