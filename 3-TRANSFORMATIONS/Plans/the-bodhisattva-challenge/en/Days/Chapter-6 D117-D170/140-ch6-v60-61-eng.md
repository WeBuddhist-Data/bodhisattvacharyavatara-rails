## Today's Verse

> If you say, "I live by gains,
> so I will exhaust my misdeeds and perform good deeds,"
> if you become angry for the sake of gains,
> will your good deeds not be exhausted and misdeeds arise? ^6-60

> If that for which I live
> is ruined,
> what is the point of a life
> that only commits misdeeds? ^6-61

## 1) Introduction to Today's Practice

Today's practice is based on the sixtieth and sixty-first verses of the sixth chapter. We want to live long so that we can do good. But if a single hatred destroys the essence of our human life completely, we will look at our own mind together today.

## 2) Commentary Explanation

The commentaries on these verses give us the following valuable quotation, which is easy to understand and encouraging. "The longer a person who practices the Dharma lives, the more their stream of good actions grows." This quotation shows that living a long time and practicing the Dharma is a cause for doing good to grow. But if we get angry for the sake of gains, merit is used up and wrongdoing grows.

## 3) Today's Practice

**Actual Practice:** Today, I will not get angry for the sake of gains, and I will practice patience.

**Explanation:** _(Patience Practice)_ If we get angry for the sake of gains, our merit is used up. If we meditate on patience, wrongdoing is used up and merit grows. This is the benefit of patience. So we must not be angry for the sake of gains, and we must practice patience.
