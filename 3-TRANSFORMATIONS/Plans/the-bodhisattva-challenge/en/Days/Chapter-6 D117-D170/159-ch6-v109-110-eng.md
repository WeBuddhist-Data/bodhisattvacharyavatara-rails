## Today's Verse

> If you say, "This enemy, without intending to cultivate patience,
> is not worthy of offering,"
> why then do you offer to the sacred teachings
> that are worthy of being a cause of accomplishment? ^6-109

> If you say, "This enemy, with the intention to harm,
> is not worthy of offering,"
> if I strive to benefit them like a doctor,
> how can my patience be accomplished? ^6-110

## 1) Introduction to Today's Practice

Today's practice is based on the one hundred and ninth and one hundred and tenth verses of the sixth chapter. Which good quality is the person you find hardest to feel grateful to helping you grow?

## 2) Commentary Explanation

The commentaries on these verses give us the following example, which is easy to understand and encouraging. If an enemy were like a good doctor who only helps us, we could not meditate on patience. A doctor wants to help us and does not harm us. Because the enemy harms us, the enemy becomes the object of our meditation on patience. The sacred Dharma also has no thought of helping us. Even so, it is a cause of good qualities, so we respect it.

## 3) Today's Practice

**Actual Practice:** Today, I will take a person who does not like me as a cause for growing patience, and I will say one gentle thing to them.

**Explanation:** _(Patience Practice)_ Even though an enemy has no wish to help, they become a cause of patience. So the person who harms us is worthy of offerings, just like the sacred Dharma. This brings peace to our own mind.
