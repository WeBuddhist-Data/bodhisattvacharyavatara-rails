## Today's Verse

> For he is not alone;
> the king's power is his power.
> Likewise, one should not despise
> any weak harm-doer. ^6-129

> For the guardians of hell
> and the compassionate ones are their allies.
> Therefore, just as common people please a cruel king,
> one should please sentient beings. ^6-130

## 1) Introduction to Today's Practice

Today's practice is based on the one hundred and twenty-ninth and one hundred and thirtieth verses of the sixth chapter. Today, who looks weak to you but has great power behind them that you cannot see?

## 2) Commentary Explanation

The commentaries on these verses give us the following example, which is easy to understand and encouraging. All of a king's power is his army. For this reason, people fear even a single general of that army. In the same way, we must not insult or beat even a weak person who harms us. The guardians of hell and the compassionate buddhas are the army of sentient beings. So, just as subjects obey a fierce king, we must work hard at ways to please sentient beings.

## 3) Today's Practice

**Actual Practice:** Today, I will not look down on any weak person or speak bad words to them.

**Explanation:** _(Avoiding wrongdoing)_ If we do not look down on the weak, there is great benefit. If we show respect to all beings, our merit grows. This makes the wish to help arise, and we gain peace and happiness.
