## Today's Verse

> For example, if a house catches fire
> and the fire spreads to another house,
> whatever grass and such it spreads to
> is removed and discarded. ^6-70

> Likewise, if the fire of hatred spreads
> to whatever one is attached to,
> fearing that one's good deeds will be consumed,
> one should immediately abandon that attachment. ^6-71

> If a condemned person has a limb cut off
> and is freed, why is that not good?
> If human suffering
> frees one from hell, why is that not good? ^6-72

## 1) Introduction to Today's Practice

Today's practice is based on the seventieth, seventy-first, and seventy-second verses of the sixth chapter. When the fire of anger blazes in your mind, what is the dry grass that you must throw out first?

## 2) Commentary Explanation

The commentaries on these verses give us the following example, which is easy to understand and encouraging. When a fire blazes in one house, the fire can spread to other houses. To keep their good household goods from burning, people quickly take out the dry grass and wood that catch fire easily, and throw them away. In the same way, attachment to people and things we desire lights the fire of hatred and burns the merit of many eons. So we must give up at once those attachments that become the cause of hatred.

## 3) Today's Practice

**Actual Practice:** Today, if anger arises because of attachment, I will put that thing down at once.

**Explanation:** _(Patience Practice)_ Desire creates anger and burns merit. If we give up the thing at once, we save our merit. This brings peace to the mind.
