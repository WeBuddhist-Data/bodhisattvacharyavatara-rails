## Today's Verse

> If you are angry at someone who harms sentient beings
> and speaks unpleasant speech,
> why are you not angry in the same way
> at someone who speaks unpleasant speech to others? ^6-62

> If you tolerate lack of faith
> in others who are dependent on it,
> why do you not tolerate unpleasant speech
> that is dependent on the arising of defilements? ^6-63

> I should not be angry
> at those who mock and destroy statues, stupas,
> or the sacred teachings,
> because the Buddhas and such are not harmed. ^6-64

## 1) Introduction to Today's Practice

Today's practice is based on the sixty-second, sixty-third, and sixty-fourth verses of the sixth chapter. When others speak bad words to us, anger blazes in our mind like a fire. We think together about this state.

## 2) Commentary Explanation

The commentaries on these verses give us the following point, which is easy to understand and encouraging. When others speak unpleasant words to us, we get angry. Suppose the reason for our anger is that those words harm our mind and our faith. Then we must also get angry when unpleasant words are spoken to another person. Why? The reason is the same. Yet it is not in agreement with reason to get angry only when bad words are said to us. This clearly shows that our anger arises just because the words are said to us.

## 3) Today's Practice

**Actual Practice:** Today, when others say bad words to me, I will think that this is being under the power of the afflictions, and I will speak gently.

**Explanation:** _(Patience Practice)_ Speaking unpleasant words is being under the power of the afflictions. If we understand this, our own anger becomes calm. This makes a good, patient mind grow.
