## Today's Verse

> Having generated the Mind of Awakening
> because all beings desire happiness,
> if beings find happiness themselves,
> why then should one be angry? ^6-80

> If you desire that beings
> worship the three worlds and attain buddhahood,
> why do you grieve
> when you see even a small amount of respect? ^6-81

> Whatever sick person you heal,
> you yourself should give to them.
> If your relatives receive sustenance,
> will you not be angry again instead of rejoicing? ^6-82

## 1) Introduction to Today's Practice

Today's practice is based on the eightieth, eighty-first, and eighty-second verses of the sixth chapter. If you have promised to give happiness to all beings, when a rival gets ahead today, will your mind become tight, or will it become relaxed?

## 2) Commentary Explanation

The commentaries on these verses give us the following example, which is easy to understand and encouraging. For example, suppose a relative whom we must support is given food and a livelihood by someone else. We do not need to give food, so we must be glad and not get angry. In the same way, if other beings find happiness without our help, we too must be pleased. We have promised to place all beings in happiness. So if they find happiness by themselves, they relieve our toil. If we get angry about this, we harm our bodhicitta.

## 3) Today's Practice

**Actual Practice:** Today, when others get ahead, I will not be jealous, and I will meditate on joy.

**Explanation:** _(Doing good)_ This calms the jealousy in our own mind. If we rejoice in the happiness of others, merit grows. This agrees with the conduct of wishing happiness for beings.
