## Today's Verse

> Because sentient beings have a part
> in the arising of the supreme Dharma of the Buddhas,
> by aligning with this much,
> sentient beings become worthy of offering. ^6-118

> Furthermore, what other way is there to repay
> those unwavering friends
> who perform immeasurable benefits,
> except by pleasing sentient beings? ^6-119

> Since one repays the benefit done to them
> by abandoning one's body and entering the lowest hell,
> therefore, even if these inflict great harm,
> one should use all goodness towards them. ^6-120

## 1) Introduction to Today's Practice

Today's practice is based on the one hundred and eighteenth, one hundred and nineteenth, and one hundred and twentieth verses of the sixth chapter. The sentient beings who hurt us today are friends whom the Buddha cared for by giving his own body and life. So how should we repay the Buddha's kindness by helping them?

## 2) Commentary Explanation

The commentaries on these verses give us the following point, which is easy to understand and encouraging. Without any deceit, the Buddha became a great friend to sentient beings he did not know. He showed them kindness beyond imagining. So the only way to repay that kindness is to make sentient beings happy. For the sake of sentient beings, the Buddha gave his own body, head, and limbs, and he even entered the Avici hell. So even if those sentient beings do us great harm, we must be good to all of them.

## 3) Today's Practice

**Actual Practice:** Today, even if other people harm me, I will speak good words to them and help them.

**Explanation:** _(Patience Practice)_ This makes the good quality of patience arise in our mind. If we help those who harm us, it is in line with the Buddha's wish. This is the practice of someone with bodhicitta.
