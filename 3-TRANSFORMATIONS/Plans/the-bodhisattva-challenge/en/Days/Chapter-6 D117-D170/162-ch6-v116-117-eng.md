## Today's Verse

> Because there is a part in the accomplishment of buddhahood,
> they are considered equal.
> No one is equal to the Buddhas,
> whose ocean of good qualities is boundless. ^6-116

> Even if a fraction of the good qualities
> of the supreme collection of good qualities
> appears in someone, to offer to them,
> even offering the three realms would be small. ^6-117

## 1) Introduction to Today's Practice

Today's practice is based on the one hundred and sixteenth and one hundred and seventeenth verses of the sixth chapter. Today, what kind of buddha seed is in the mind of the person who makes you angry?

## 2) Commentary Explanation

The commentaries on these verses give us the following point, which is easy to understand and encouraging. Sentient beings have one part of the causes from which a buddha's qualities, such as the powers, arise. So from that side, sentient beings and buddhas are counted as equal. But the omniscient Buddha, who has an ocean of endless good qualities, and sentient beings are not equal at all. Only a buddha has every supreme good quality in full. If even a small part of a buddha's qualities appears in a person's mind, offering them all three worlds would still be too little.

## 3) Today's Practice

**Actual Practice:** Today, if another person makes me angry, I will remember that this person also has the cause of buddhahood.

**Explanation:** _(Taming the mind)_ All sentient beings equally have the cause of buddhahood. By thinking like this, our anger calms down. This makes loving-kindness grow in our mind.
