## Today's Verse

> If I grasp a boil
> that resembles a human form,
> is unbearable and painful,
> at whom should I be angry for the harm? ^6-44

> Foolish people do not desire suffering,
> yet they crave the causes of suffering.
> Therefore, when harm comes from their own faults,
> what is there to blame others for? ^6-45

> Just like the guardians of hell
> and the sword-leaf forest,
> at whom should I be angry
> when this is created by my own actions? ^6-46

## 1) Introduction to Today's Practice

Today's practice is based on the forty-fourth, forty-fifth, and forty-sixth verses of the sixth chapter. When someone hits our wound with a stick, we do not look at our own wound. We get angry at the other person. Today we must examine this mistake.

## 2) Commentary Explanation

The commentaries on these verses give us the following valuable example, which is easy to understand and encouraging. The guardians of hell and the forest of sword leaves are illusions created by our own bad karma. In the same way, an enemy harms our body and mind in this life. This harm is also created by our own bad karma from the past. So it is not right to be angry at anyone who harms us. If we do not want the result of suffering, we must give up its cause.

## 3) Today's Practice

**Actual Practice:** Today, when others harm me, I will think about my own karma and give up anger.

**Explanation:** _(Patience Practice)_ The harm that others do to us comes from our own karma. So it is not right to be angry at others. If we give up anger, the cause of suffering ends and our mind gains peace.
