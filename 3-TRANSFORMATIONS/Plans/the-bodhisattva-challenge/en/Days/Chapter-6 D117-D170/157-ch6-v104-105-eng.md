## Today's Verse

> That without which something does not arise,
> and when it exists, that something exists—
> if that is its cause,
> how can it be called a hindrance? ^6-104

> A beggar who arrives on time
> is not a hindrance to generosity.
> One who causes renunciation
> is not inappropriately called a hindrance to renunciation. ^6-105

## 1) Introduction to Today's Practice

Today's practice is based on the one hundred and fourth and one hundred and fifth verses of the sixth chapter. Like a beggar who arrives when we are giving, is the person we get angry at an obstacle to patience?

## 2) Commentary Explanation

The commentaries on these verses give us the following example, which is easy to understand and encouraging. When we are giving, if a beggar comes, the beggar is a cause of giving. So we cannot say that the beggar blocks our giving. In the same way, an enemy is a cause for meditating on patience. So the enemy also cannot block our merit.

## 3) Today's Practice

**Actual Practice:** Today, when a difficult person gets angry, I will take it as a condition for meditating on patience, and I will keep my mind relaxed.

**Explanation:** _(Patience Practice)_ An enemy is a condition for patience to arise. The person who harms us makes merit grow. The anger in the mind becomes calm.
