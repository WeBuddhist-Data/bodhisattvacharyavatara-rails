## Today's Verse

> Even towards those who harm
> gurus, relatives, and friends,
> seeing that it arises from conditions in the same way as before,
> turn away from anger. ^6-65

> If both sentient and inanimate things
> cause harm,
> why single out sentient beings for blame?
> Therefore, endure harm. ^6-66

> Some commit faults out of ignorance.
> If some become angry out of ignorance,
> who is faultless in that?
> Who is it that is flawed? ^6-67

## 1) Introduction to Today's Practice

Today's practice is based on the sixty-fifth, sixty-sixth, and sixty-seventh verses of the sixth chapter. When our teacher or a close friend says bad words and hurts us, we think this: both the one who harms and the one who gets angry have fallen under the power of ignorance.

## 2) Commentary Explanation

The commentaries on these verses give us the following point, which is easy to understand and encouraging. Other people harm people. Things that have no mind, such as illness, fire, and water, also harm people. Both kinds of harm arise from causes and conditions, so neither is independent. So it is not reasonable to be angry at the person alone. We must accept all harm and meditate on patience.

## 3) Today's Practice

**Actual Practice:** Today, if those close to me or my friends harm me, I will think that it comes from conditions, and I will not get angry.

**Explanation:** _(Patience Practice)_ This practice brings peace to the mind. All harm arises from conditions. If we turn back anger, it helps both ourselves and others.
