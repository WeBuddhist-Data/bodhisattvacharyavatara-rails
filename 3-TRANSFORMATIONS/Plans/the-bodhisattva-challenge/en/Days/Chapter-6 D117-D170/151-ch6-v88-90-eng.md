## Today's Verse

> If that suffering is accomplished by your desire,
> what is there for you to be glad about?
> If you say, "I will be satisfied,"
> what greater ruin is there than that? ^6-88

> This hook cast by the fisherman of defilements
> is an unbearable dart.
> Seized by it, I am cooked by the guardians of hell
> in the pot of hell. ^6-89

> Praise and the respect of fame
> do not become merit, nor do they greatly increase it.
> My strength does not increase, nor am I free from illness.
> Nor does my body become happy. ^6-90

## 1) Introduction to Today's Practice

Today's practice is based on the eighty-eighth, eighty-ninth, and ninetieth verses of the sixth chapter. If the afflictions catch the mind like a fisherman's hook, and praise does not give us life or merit, what does praise really give us after the joy fades?

## 2) Commentary Explanation

The commentaries on these verses give us the following example, which is easy to understand and encouraging. The fisherman of the afflictions throws a sharp hook of harmful karma. Once that hook catches us, the guardians of hell cook us in the copper pot of hell. Praise and fame do not increase merit for the next life. They do not bring happiness or a long life in this life. They do not give the body strength or freedom from illness either. So there is no reason at all to be glad about them.

## 3) Today's Practice

**Actual Practice:** Today, when other people praise me, I will not be attached, and I will stay even-minded.

**Explanation:** _(Taming the mind)_ Praise does not increase merit or life. If we are not attached to it, the mind gets rest.
