## Today's Verse

> This pleases the Buddhas.
> This also truly accomplishes my own purpose.
> This also removes the suffering of the world.
> Therefore, I will always do this. ^6-127

> For example, if a king's attendant
> harms many people,
> people with foresight
> do not harm him in return, even if they could. ^6-128

## 1) Introduction to Today's Practice

Today's practice is based on the one hundred and twenty-seventh and one hundred and twenty-eighth verses of the sixth chapter. Today, who is standing quietly behind the person who annoys you?

## 2) Commentary Explanation

The commentaries on these verses give us the following example, which is easy to understand and encouraging. An official sent by the king may harm the people of a city. Even so, people who see far ahead do not harm him back, even though they have the power. This is because they fear the danger that will come in the future. In the same way, respecting sentient beings is a cause that pleases the buddhas. It gathers merit for us and clears away the suffering of the world. So we must always show respect without stopping.

## 3) Today's Practice

**Actual Practice:** Today, I will speak to everyone I meet with gentle and respectful words.

**Explanation:** _(Doing good)_ If we respect others, the buddhas become pleased. Through this, our own purpose is truly fulfilled. It clears away the suffering of the world.
