## Today's Verse

> Driven by my own actions,
> those who harm me arise.
> If they go to hell because of this,
> have I not ruined them? ^6-47

> By relying on these,
> my misdeeds greatly increase through patience.
> By relying on me, they
> will go to hell for a long time, suffering. ^6-48

## 1) Introduction to Today's Practice

Today's practice is based on the forty-seventh and forty-eighth verses of the sixth chapter. Someone makes us angry. This gives us a chance to meditate on patience. But our anger sends them into suffering.

## 2) Commentary Explanation

The commentaries on these verses give us the following valuable point, which is easy to understand and encouraging. If we meditate on patience by relying on someone who harms us, a lot of wrongdoing is purified and used up. We gathered this wrongdoing in earlier lives. Meditating on patience does not only purify wrongdoing. It also completes a great store of merit. So the enemy has helped us. Because of the harm they do to us, those enemies will go to hell in later lives. They will suffer there for a long time. These enemies are our companions in meditating on patience and in purifying wrongdoing. So they are the ones who help us.

## 3) Today's Practice

**Actual Practice:** Today, if someone harms me, I will meditate on patience, which purifies wrongdoing and completes the store of merit. I will not get angry.

**Explanation:** _(Patience Practice)_ Because of someone who harms us, our wrongdoing is purified. Meditating on patience completes our merit. So the one who harms us is helping us.
